Supergirl Storyline & Honest Review

James Gunn's DCU takes its boldest swing yet with Supergirl, and Milly Alcock absolutely owns it. Where Superman was all heart, Kara Zor-El is all edge — a hero shaped by a far harsher Kryptonian upbringing, played with a wry, rebellious charm that never tips into caricature. Director Craig Gillespie (I, Tonya, Cruella) leans into the cosmic road-movie structure of Tom King and Bilquis Evely's acclaimed Woman of Tomorrow comic, and it is the film's best decision. The space-opera scope feels genuinely fresh for the genre, the action has real weight, and Jason Momoa's Lobo is pure chaotic fun every second he is on screen. It is not flawless — the middle act sags slightly under the weight of its own mythology — but the emotional payoff lands hard. The tagline says it all: "Truth. Justice. Whatever." This is a superhero film with actual attitude, and Alcock announces herself as a star.
