Everything Everywhere All at Once Storyline & Honest Review

Everything Everywhere All at Once is the most wildly inventive film of the decade — a multiverse epic wrapped around a laundromat owner doing her taxes. Michelle Yeoh plays Evelyn Wang, who discovers she must channel versions of herself from parallel universes to save existence itself. Directors Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert (collectively "Daniels") throw everything at the screen: martial-arts mayhem, absurdist comedy, genuine romance, and philosophical depth about kindness in a chaotic world. The film's secret weapon is that beneath the multiverse madness beats a deeply human story about a fractured family finding its way back to each other. Yeoh gives a career-best performance, Ke Huy Quan's comeback turn won him an Oscar, and Stephanie Hsu is a revelation as both daughter and destroyer of worlds. It swept seven Academy Awards including Best Picture — proof that originality still wins.
