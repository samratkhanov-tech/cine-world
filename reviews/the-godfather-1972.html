The Godfather Storyline & Honest Review

Francis Ford Coppola's The Godfather is widely considered the greatest film ever made — and for good reason. The story of the Corleone crime family, led by the aging Don Vito Corleone (Marlon Brando) and his reluctant heir Michael (Al Pacino), is Shakespearean in scope and intimate in emotion. What sets The Godfather apart is its patience. Every scene breathes. The wedding sequence that opens the film runs nearly half an hour, yet it establishes character, power dynamics, and theme without a wasted frame. Brando's mumbling, cotton-mouthed Don is iconic, but it's Pacino's chilling transformation from war hero to ruthless boss that gives the film its tragic spine. Gordon Willis's shadow-drenched cinematography and Nino Rota's haunting score complete a film that rewards repeat viewings like no other.

Who directed The Godfather?
The Godfather was directed by Francis Ford Coppola, based on Mario Puzo's bestselling novel.
