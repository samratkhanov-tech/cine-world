Joker Storyline & Honest Review

Todd Phillips' Joker is a disturbing character study that reimagines Gotham's most infamous villain as Arthur Fleck (Joaquin Phoenix), a failed comedian crushed by a society that refuses to see him. Phoenix's Oscar-winning performance is a physical transformation — all angular limbs, pained laughter, and slow-burning rage. The film borrows its grimy 1970s Gotham aesthetic from Scorsese classics like Taxi Driver, but its power comes from how uncomfortably it mirrors real anxieties about isolation and inequality. Hildur Guðnadóttir's cello-heavy score won an Oscar and crawls under your skin from the opening frames. Whether you see it as a cautionary tale or a character tragedy, Joker is impossible to shake — a comic-book film that plays like an arthouse gut-punch.
