Runner Storyline & Honest Review

Some movies win you over with originality. Others win you over with sheer momentum. Runner (2026) is firmly in the second camp — a 98-minute sprint that pairs Alan Ritchson's battering-ram physicality with Owen Wilson's hangdog charm and never once hits the brakes. It is not a great film. It is a great ride. Hank Malone (Alan Ritchson) is an ex-soldier trying to outrun his past. Ben Bishop (Owen Wilson) is a medical courier with a simple job: transport a rare donor organ across the city to a terminally ill young girl waiting for a transplant. When a cartel crew led by the ruthless Damian Zaldívar (Rodrigo Santoro) decides that organ — or the people carrying it — is worth killing for, the delivery becomes a desperate race against time. Every mile brings a new ambush, every shortcut a new trap, and the clock on a dying girl's life keeps ticking. Alan Ritchson continues his reign as Hollywood's most believable human wrecking ball. As Hank Malone, he brings the same coiled intensity that made him a star — but here he gets to play the weariness too, a soldier tired of being a weapon. Owen Wilson is the film's secret weapon: his wide-eyed panic against Ritchson's stone-faced resolve generates the film's best laughs, and the odd-couple chemistry between them is the reason the movie works. Rodrigo Santoro makes for a smooth, menacing cartel boss, while Leila George and Adriana Barraza keep the human stakes front and center. Director Scott Waugh knows his way around a chase sequence, and Runner plays entirely to his strengths. The action is practical, punchy, and refreshingly legible — car chases that obey the laws of physics, hand-to-hand fights with real weight, and set pieces staged around the ticking-clock premise so every stunt means something. At 98 minutes, the film never overstays its welcome. What works: the lead chemistry, the propulsive pacing, clean and exciting action choreography, and a premise with genuine emotional stakes. What doesn't: the story is thin and predictable — you will guess most twists early, the villains are broad strokes, and the script plays it safe. But audiences (90% on Rotten Tomatoes) got it exactly right: this is a crowd-pleaser, and our verdict is a solid 7.0/10 — an easy Friday-night recommendation.
